The transaction-cost-aware model is trained by including implementation costs directly in the objective function. This differs from an ex-post cost analysis because the trading policy can adapt during training to reduce costly rebalancing.

The transaction-cost-aware strategy reaches a net-error MSE of 0.0870, compared with 0.0934 for the MSE baseline and 0.0919 for the asymmetric loss. Its average net error is -0.2509, with a 5% CVaR of -0.6677.

The main effect of including transaction costs in the objective is a clear reduction in trading intensity. Average turnover decreases from 2.5425 for the MSE baseline to 1.5830, and average transaction costs decrease from 0.0509 to 0.0317.

This improvement in implementation cost comes with a trade-off. The gross hedging-error MSE increases from 0.0669 under the MSE baseline to 0.0711 under the transaction-cost-aware model. Moreover, the asymmetric-loss model remains slightly better in downside-risk terms, with a CVaR of -0.6403 against -0.6677 for the transaction-cost-aware model.

Overall, the transaction-cost-aware strategy confirms the expected economic mechanism: by internalizing frictions during training, the model learns a less aggressive trading policy. The resulting hedge is cheaper to implement and improves net-error MSE, but it sacrifices part of the gross replication accuracy and does not fully dominate the asymmetric-loss strategy in tail-risk protection.
