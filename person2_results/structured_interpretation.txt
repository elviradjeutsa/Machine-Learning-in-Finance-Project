The two strategies display very similar hedging-error MSE values: 0.0669 for the baseline and 0.0672 for the asymmetric-loss specification. This indicates that the alternative loss does not materially improve average replication accuracy.

In net PnL terms, the baseline has mean -0.2710 and standard deviation 0.1412, while the asymmetric-loss strategy has mean -0.2689 and standard deviation 0.1399. The two specifications therefore remain close in average performance.

The main difference appears in downside risk. The 5% net-PnL quantile improves from -0.5391 under the baseline to -0.5163 under the asymmetric loss, while the corresponding CVaR improves from -0.6739 to -0.6403. This shows that the asymmetric loss reduces the severity of adverse outcomes.

Average transaction costs also decrease from 0.0509 to 0.0489, and average turnover decreases from 2.5425 to 2.4438. The asymmetric-loss strategy is therefore associated with a slightly less aggressive trading policy.

Overall, the change in loss function affects the risk profile of the strategy more than its average MSE performance. The asymmetric specification leads to a more conservative hedge, with improved downside protection and slightly lower implementation costs.
